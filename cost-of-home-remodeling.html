If you're planning a home remodel in Fort Worth this year, one of the first questions you'll ask is: how much is this going to cost? The honest answer depends on the scope of your project, materials, and labor — but we can give you real numbers based on what we're seeing in 2026.

WHAT DRIVES REMODELING COSTS IN FORT WORTH?

Remodeling costs in DFW have shifted significantly over the past few years. Material costs, labor demand, and supply chain factors all play a role. In 2026, Fort Worth homeowners should expect:

  • Scope of work — cosmetic updates vs. structural changes
  • Materials selected — stock vs. custom, budget vs. premium
  • Labor — licensed and insured contractors vs. unlicensed crews
  • Permits — required for structural, plumbing, and electrical work
  • Project timeline — phased projects vs. full gut renovations

KITCHEN REMODELING COSTS

Kitchen remodels remain the most popular home improvement project in Fort Worth — and for good reason. A well-executed kitchen renovation typically returns 60–80% of its cost in home value.

💡 Alonso Tip:

Keeping your existing kitchen layout — where your sink, stove, and refrigerator currently sit — can save you $5,000–$15,000 by avoiding plumbing and electrical relocation costs.

BATHROOM REMODELING COSTS

Bathroom remodels in Fort Worth range widely depending on size and finish level. A primary bathroom renovation with a walk-in shower, new vanity, tile floors, and updated fixtures typically runs $18,000–$35,000.

HOME ADDITIONS

Adding square footage to your Fort Worth home is a major investment but one of the most impactful ways to increase both livable space and property value. Room additions typically start around $40,000–$80,000.

HOW TO BUDGET WISELY

  • Get at least 3 itemized bids from licensed contractors
  • Add a 10–15% contingency buffer for unexpected costs
  • Prioritize projects with the highest ROI first
  • Ask your contractor about phased timelines if budget is a concern
  • Verify that your contractor is licensed, insured, and pulls proper permits
⚠️ Watch Out For:

Bids that seem significantly lower than others are often a red flag. Unlicensed contractors who skip permits can leave you with work that fails inspection — and becomes your financial responsibility.
